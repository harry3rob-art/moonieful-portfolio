The problem

Burgland's founder spent her career in electrical engineering, Department of Defense software contracts and bank technology at USAA, then formed Burgland Technology Partners in 2023 to bring that experience to growing businesses. As she got ready for her first clients, she named the problems she faced in her own words: “identifying the right customers, funding and scaling the business.”

What we did

After our review in March 2024, we delivered her full brand: a logo and brand kit, 200 printed business cards, a retractable banner, Facebook graphics, stickers and a website with a client login and dashboard. What it taught usBurgland's founder asked for help finding the right customers, and we answered with a polished brand kit and general advice, so now we ask who buys before we design anything.

Mission-driven Education

SSG Educational

Trauma-informed teaching methodology · 2024 to 2025

The problem

SSG's founder spent more than 40 years in education, including years as a teacher and principal inside a juvenile detention school, and she sums up her method in four words: “I teach the teachers.” Teachers use her method every day, but the contract comes from school district leaders, who decide which training their teachers get.

What we did

In February 2025 we handed her a brand board and logo, letterhead, a trifold brochure, a retractable banner, social graphics, stickers, and website icons and photos. Alongside the visuals we wrote her a marketing and copywriting guide with homepage copy, welcome emails and an SEO keyword sheet aimed at school districts, then sat down with her again in March for a one-hour review.

What it taught usTeachers use SSG's method while district leaders pay for it, which taught us that the person you serve and the person who signs can be different people.

Workforce Training · B2B

TECHSIS

Fiber optic career training · Texas · September 2026

The problem

The founder of TECHSIS brings 35 years of military telecom and fiber engineering to a one-week broadband bootcamp, but a workforce board reading his website couldn't tell whether TECHSIS was a training school, a sales company or a grant partner.

What we did

We named the buyer who signs the check, the workforce development board, and rebuilt the Light NU pitch around the first question that buyer asks: are participants still working 90 days later? From there we wrote and designed a new homepage, wrote the student page and laid out four pricing options for his first partner meetings. Our SnapGist scan scored his old homepage three out of 10 for clarity, and the new one scores eight.

Youth Nonprofit

Sincerra Kids

A Courtney Wilson Enterprises nonprofit · Western New York · October 2026

The problem

Sincerra Kids' founder started the nonprofit so talented Western New York kids don't have to quit sports and the arts over fees, but her website spoke only to donors, so the families she started it for had no way to apply.

What we did

We split the site into two paths: a homepage written for parents, with the application up front, and its own giving page for the local companies and individual donors who pay for each season. We wrote the copy, designed the pages and built a pitch deck she can bring to sponsors. Her new headline names the exact fees kids are quitting over, so a parent knows in one line whether Sincerra Kids can help.
